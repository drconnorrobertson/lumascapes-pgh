Price the same brief before comparing the totals

Reviewed November 22, 2026 · Lumascapes

A fixture count leaves several questions unanswered: which parts of the property are included, how the proposed equipment will be reached and connected, what is excluded, and who returns to review the scene after dark. Write down those differences before asking why two proposals have different totals.

This guide is a scope-planning exercise. It does not publish Lumascapes prices or estimate an installation from a photograph. Use the installation cost guide for the broader discussion and the quote checklist when reviewing a written proposal.

A fictional eight-fixture comparison

Suppose a homeowner wants an entry route, a patio, and two trees considered. The two fictional concepts below each list eight fixtures. The quantities are invented to illustrate a scope difference; neither concept is a design recommendation, an actual quote, or a completed project.

Concept A leaves the patio out. Concept B allocates fewer fixtures to the entry. Neither count tells you whether a route is adequately lit, whether an exposed light source is uncomfortable, or whether the equipment and controls are equivalent. Ask for the intended result and inclusions to be described; do not divide the total by eight and treat that as a comparable unit price.

Resolve six scope differences

  1. Coverage: mark each entry, path, gathering area, and feature included. Identify requested areas left out.
  2. Equipment: ask for the proposed fixture, light-source, transformer, and control details. A shared fixture count does not establish matching equipment.
  3. Route and access: identify paved crossings, planting beds, walls, and areas the installer has not inspected. Ask who confirms a workable route; do not assume a line on a sketch establishes access.
  4. Existing system: record equipment proposed for reuse and who assesses compatibility and condition. Keep unverified reuse as an open question.
  5. Finishing and follow-up: clarify surface restoration, debris removal, nighttime aiming, and what any return visit includes. Separate these from ongoing maintenance.
  6. Changes and exclusions: ask what happens if a concealed condition changes the scope, how additional work is approved, and which items remain outside the quoted total.

Use an uncertainty list rather than an assumed allowance

For example, if the route across a patio has not been reviewed, write “crossing method and restoration responsibility unconfirmed.” Ask the installer to explain how that uncertainty is addressed in the proposal. Do not enter zero simply because a separate line item is absent, or assume that one quote includes the same work as another.

Download a blank scope comparison record (CSV). Record each proposal's wording, the open question, the person confirming it, and the dated answer. Keep the original proposal with the record.

Discuss the lighting result as well as the installation

The DarkSky and Illuminating Engineering Society principles emphasize a defined purpose, directing light where needed, restrained output, appropriate controls, and warmer light where possible. Ask how the proposed scenes address those aims. These principles do not supply a fixture count, price, or local approval for your property.

If work must be staged, use the whole-property phasing example to separate the first phase from later work. Discuss the complete brief with Lumascapes installation services or start a project conversation.
