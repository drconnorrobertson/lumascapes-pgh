9 Checks for Pittsburgh Landscape Lighting Quotes

Landscape lighting quotes are difficult to compare when one lists fixtures and another describes a finished nighttime scene. Start with the routes and views you want to improve. Then ask each provider to explain how its proposal addresses those same priorities on your Pittsburgh-area property.

Work through the decision

  1. Agree on the project areas

    Mark the entry route, steps, patio and focal points included in each proposal. Identify areas reserved for a future phase. A smaller quote may cover fewer views, so compare the plan boundary before judging the price or the fixture count.

  2. Request a design explanation

    Ask what each group of fixtures is intended to accomplish and where the light will stop. Discuss the view from the street, your windows and neighboring property. More brightness is not a substitute for a plan that makes the intended route or feature legible.

  3. Identify actual fixtures

    Request manufacturer and model information, finish, light-source details and the proposed accessories. Ask what can be serviced or replaced later. Generic descriptions such as premium brass do not tell you which product is included or whether a replacement will match the original installation.

  4. Clarify installation conditions

    Describe slopes, planting beds, retaining walls, established roots and hardscape crossings during the walkthrough. Ask which restoration work is included. A quote based only on photographs should identify the site assumptions that could change after the installer inspects the property.

  5. Compare controls and operating periods

    Ask how the system will switch on and off, who sets the schedule and how the homeowner changes it. Discuss whether separate areas need separate operating periods. The control arrangement should fit actual evening use rather than leaving every feature illuminated all night.

  6. Include nighttime aiming

    Ask whether the scope includes reviewing the installed effect after dark and adjusting the aiming or shielding. Daytime placement alone does not show glare from a driver's seat or light entering a bedroom window. Define how a concern found during that review will be resolved.

  7. Separate product and labor coverage

    Request written warranty terms and the person responsible for claims. Ask whether a product replacement also includes diagnostic visits, labor and shipping. A manufacturer's product warranty and the installer's service commitment can cover different parts of the same issue.

  8. Plan for growing plants

    Ask how fixtures can be accessed, cleaned and adjusted as the garden changes. Identify areas likely to need seasonal attention. The initial design should leave a practical maintenance route rather than placing a serviceable part where it becomes difficult to reach.

  9. Get an installed-system record

    Request fixture locations, product identifiers, control instructions and a contact for service. Record the agreed nighttime adjustments before closing the punch list. These details make later expansion and maintenance easier even if you eventually hire a different service provider.

Worked example

Illustrative comparison: one proposal covers only the front facade, while another includes the walk, two grade changes and a patio control zone. Ask for aligned alternatives covering the same areas. A fixture-per-dollar comparison would miss the different installation work and the functions served by those lights.

Your next step

Take daytime photographs and note where you walk, sit and look after sunset. Send the same marked plan to each bidder. Keep a comparison record of scope, product details, installation assumptions, nighttime review and service exclusions before accepting a proposal.

Common questions

Can I compare quotes by fixture count?

Fixture count is one input. Beam control, placement, installation conditions and the intended effect also matter, so compare the whole written scope.

Should every light stay on all night?

Discuss a schedule matched to the purpose of each area. Useful lighting can be controlled so that it operates when needed.
