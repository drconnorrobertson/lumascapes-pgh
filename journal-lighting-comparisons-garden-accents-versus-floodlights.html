Garden accents versus floodlights for Pittsburgh-area homes

Selective accents create layers, while a floodlight tends to flatten large areas.

Define which features deserve attention. For Pittsburgh-area homes, this planning question belongs within a complete view of the house, outdoor route, and garden. Begin with the feature you want to improve and the viewpoint from which you will experience it.

What to notice on your property

Define which features deserve attention. Walk the area after dark and note where you approach, pause, or look through a window. A design that appears balanced in a daytime drawing may feel different at night because surrounding areas fall into shadow.

Notice surfaces that can reflect or absorb light, nearby trees that may grow into a beam, and any view toward a neighbor. These details help make the finished scene comfortable as well as beautiful.

Compare the options for garden accents versus floodlights

Show the places you want to use and the view you want to improve. Ask what each proposed light is doing in the composition. For garden accents versus floodlights, that conversation should connect the specific feature with the house, the garden, and any route through the space.

Consider how the scene may change over time. Plants grow, seasons shift, and fixtures sometimes need to be adjusted. A plan with service access in mind is easier to keep looking intentional.

A practical next step

Take a few photographs from the street, the main route, and the most important indoor view. Share them with a short description of what currently feels dark, too bright, or unfinished. That gives a lighting designer a more useful starting point than a fixture count alone.
