Architectural Landscape Lighting for Pittsburgh-area homes

Stone, brick, timber, and thoughtful details deserve more than a bright fixture at the front door. Architectural landscape lighting uses placement and restraint to reveal those details from the curb and from the landscape around them.

Every property and project is different. Our conversation begins with what you want to see, how you use the space, and which parts of the home or landscape deserve attention after dark.

Start with the architecture

Every home has its own rhythm. An entry arch might be the natural focal point; another property may call for a softer wash across brick or stone. A balanced design considers where the viewer stands, how the light reaches a surface, and what remains in shadow. The result should feel intentional from several angles, not only in a single photograph.

Connect the house to the landscape

Architecture and plantings look best when they belong to the same composition. Illuminating a tree canopy or a textured wall can add depth beyond the front facade. Paths and steps then lead the eye through the scene while making the route easier to follow.

Think beyond the first night

Landscapes grow, seasons change, and fixtures may need attention. A lighting plan should be easy to revisit as trees mature or the way you use the property changes. Lumascapes lists service as part of its offering, so an existing design can be discussed along with a new one.

The best lighting gives every evening a more considered point of view.

What a first conversation can cover

  • The spaces you want to use, highlight, or navigate after sunset
  • How the lighting should look from the street and from inside the home
  • Whether an existing system is present and what you would like to change
  • Your location in the Pittsburgh area and the next practical step

Common questions

Will uplighting make my home too bright?

It does not have to. Fixture placement, beam spread, and aiming can create a soft, selective effect rather than blanket brightness.

Does architectural lighting work on brick or stone?

Both can respond beautifully to directional light. The right approach depends on the surface texture, color, and viewing angle.

Can trees be part of the design?

Yes. Lighting a tree or planting can give the view depth and keep the house from feeling isolated against a dark yard.
