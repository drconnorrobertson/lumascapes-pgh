Lighting a sloped Pittsburgh yard for Pittsburgh homes.

Grade changes create both dramatic views and unusual glare angles.

Record the view from each level of the yard

Reviewed November 22, 2026 · Lumascapes

A hillside brief should describe more than the view from the upper deck. Include the lower approach, the transitions between levels, and the view from inside. These are separate observation points: a concept that looks appealing from one point still needs to be reviewed from the others.

This is a homeowner observation and consultation worksheet, not a fixture-placement plan. Use existing accessible routes in conditions you can navigate comfortably. Record an inaccessible area as “not observed” and arrange an on-site review; the worksheet does not call for climbing a slope, entering a neighbor's property, or moving electrical equipment.

A fictional three-viewpoint brief

Imagine an upper deck, steps to a lower gate, and a room facing the yard. This is an invented planning example, not a Lumascapes installation. There are no measured light levels or observed results. Before a demonstration, all comfort and visibility findings remain untested.

Three observations to request for the same concept
ViewpointPurpose in this fictional briefCheck during an agreed demonstrationCurrent finding
V1 — upper deck, standingSee the route toward the lower gateCan the route and level changes be distinguished without an uncomfortable exposed source?Untested
V2 — lower gate, facing uphillApproach the steps and upper landingWhat is visible when looking toward the higher level? Does a source face this viewpoint?Untested
V3 — inside room, seatedEnjoy the yard through the windowHow do the yard, reflections, and visible light sources appear with normal indoor lights on?Untested

Give every photograph a viewpoint label

Make a simple sketch linking V1, V2, and V3 to the property. Record the direction you face, whether you are standing or seated, the date, weather, existing lights in use, and the photograph filename. A daytime photo can show the route and planting; an evening photo records a different condition. Keep both labels rather than treating them as interchangeable evidence.

Phone exposure can change how bright a scene appears. Use photos to locate concerns and explain the brief, not as a substitute for agreed on-site observations or required measurements. The nighttime walkthrough guide helps organize the review.

Separate a concern from a tested finding

  • Concern: “A light above the lower gate may be visible from below.” This is a question to test, not a recorded glare failure.
  • Observed finding: record the viewpoint, conditions, and what the person actually saw during the demonstration. Avoid substituting a daytime drawing for that observation.
  • Follow-up: identify the change to review, who will review it, and the unresolved point. Retest the affected viewpoints rather than closing an issue because one view improved.

Download the blank viewpoint record (CSV). Leave unknown conditions marked unknown. The blank file contains no customer information or prefilled passing results.

Put site constraints on the same sketch

Mark walls, existing steps, planting, accessible routes, and equipment you know about; distinguish confirmed features from assumed routes. Ask the installer to assess access and the proposed connection route. Discuss planned planting or construction that could change the view. A lighting observation does not establish that a retaining wall, stair, or electrical installation is compliant or sound.

For discussion of light direction, shielding, output, and controls, see the DarkSky/IES responsible-lighting principles. Use them to frame questions about where light is needed and where it should be limited; they do not certify this fictional concept or replace site-specific requirements.

Bring a short, usable brief

Send the labeled sketch, a few relevant photos, the routes and views you care about, and the unanswered questions. Review path and step lighting, deck lighting, and the scope comparison example when discussing priorities. Contact Lumascapes to confirm an appropriate review for the property.
