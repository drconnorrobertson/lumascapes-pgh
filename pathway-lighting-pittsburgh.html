Pathway and Step Lighting for Pittsburgh-area homes

A path is more than a line from the driveway to the front door. In the evening it becomes part of the experience of arriving home. A thoughtful mix of path lights, step lights, and nearby landscape accents can make the route clear without a row of glaring fixtures.

Every property and project is different. Our conversation begins with what you want to see, how you use the space, and which parts of the home or landscape deserve attention after dark.

Light the route, not every inch

Good path lighting reveals the next step and the direction of travel. That often means focusing on turns, level changes, edges, and transitions rather than trying to cover every square foot equally. The surrounding landscape can provide visual cues too.

Work with Pittsburgh terrain

Many Pittsburgh-area properties include slopes, steps, retaining walls, and established planting. Those features can make an entry memorable, but they also change how light is seen from above and below. A design should be evaluated from the street, the driveway, the walk, and the doorway.

Keep fixtures part of the landscape

Fixture scale and placement matter during daylight as well as at night. The goal is to support the path and the architecture while avoiding an overly repetitive look. As planting grows, occasional adjustments may help preserve the original effect.

The best lighting gives every evening a more considered point of view.

What a first conversation can cover

  • The spaces you want to use, highlight, or navigate after sunset
  • How the lighting should look from the street and from inside the home
  • Whether an existing system is present and what you would like to change
  • Your location in the Pittsburgh area and the next practical step

Common questions

How far apart should path lights be?

There is no one spacing rule for every property. Path shape, fixture output, nearby planting, and steps all affect the layout.

Are step lights and path lights the same?

They serve related purposes but create different effects. Step lights focus on a level change; path lights often illuminate the walking surface and nearby planting.

Can lighting be added to an existing walkway?

Often, yes. The available layout depends on access, existing materials, and the route for wiring. A site conversation is the right first step.
